    Chapter 15: Family Planning (FP)¶

    15.1 Family Planning Service Provision¶

    15.1.1 Provide Information about FP including Pre-Conception Care to Different Groups¶

    The procedures used here are also used in the next step to recruit clients for family planning and maternal health services in young child clinics, antenatal clinics, labour and delivery wards, outpatient departments, outreach services, postpartum clinics, and in providing education on specific chosen family planning methods.

    The objective is to:

    • Create awareness.
    • Disseminate correct information to influence people to change beliefs, attitudes, and practices.
    • Recruit new clients and offer several family planning methods.

    15.1.2 Counsel High-Risk Clients¶

    Risk factors to look out for in clients include:

    • Recent delivery or abortion
    • More than 4 pregnancies
    • Age above 35 years or below 20 years
    • Complicating medical conditions, such as diabetes or heart disease
    • Living with HIV/AIDS
    • Having children with birth interval below 2 years
    • Poor obstetric history that is likely to recur in future pregnancies, such as postpartum haemorrhage or pre-eclampsia

    Eligible non-pregnant women can be identified while conducting services in:

    • Young child clinics and paediatric wards
    • Maternity and postnatal clinics and wards
    • Outpatient clinics
    • Youth and adolescent centres
    • HIV/AIDS care centres and ART clinics
    • Sexual and reproductive health clinics, such as cervical cancer clinics, post-abortion care clinics, and adolescent/youth clinics
    • Male clinics
    • Gender-based violence clinics or corners

    Eligible women can also be identified while conducting outreaches, including immunisation outreaches or home visits.

    Note

    Discuss reproductive choices and risk factors with clients. Give special consideration to first-time parents and adolescents by providing appropriate information on sexuality, family planning, and family planning services, including types, benefits, availability, and procedures.

    15.1.3 Pre-Conception Care with Clients Who Desire to Conceive¶

    Pre-conception care discussion topics for clients who desire to conceive include:

    • Pregnancy planning and appropriate contraception
    • Folic acid supplementation for 3 months preceding conception
    • Good diet
    • Risk assessment and management of pre-existing conditions and risk factors
    • Benefits of pre-conception care, such as prevention of unintended pregnancies and improved maternal and foetal outcomes
    • Screening for hereditary diseases, such as sickle cell disease
    • Screening for STIs, including HIV and hepatitis

    15.1.4 Discuss with People Living with HIV: Special Considerations for HIV Transmission¶

    Key areas for discussion include:

    • Prevention of HIV transmission to spouse and child
    • Safer sexual practices and safe conception
    • Education and counselling about perinatal transmission risk
    • Initiation or modification of ART, considering toxicity
    • Evaluation of opportunistic infections and offering appropriate management
    • Possible interaction between some ARV drugs and hormonal contraceptives, which may reduce contraceptive effect
    • Use of additional barrier methods, such as condoms, which also prevent STIs

    15.1.5 Educate and Counsel Clients to Make an Informed Choice of FP Method¶

    The primary objectives are:

    • To dispel rumours and misconceptions about family planning.
    • To help the client make a voluntary informed choice.

    Procedure

    • Prepare the room and materials needed, ensuring privacy.
    • Assess the client’s knowledge and experience of family planning methods.
    • Explain the different family planning methods available, including:
    • Type
    • Mechanism of action and method of use
    • Advantages and disadvantages
    • Indications
    • Contraindications
    • Side effects
    • Complications and warning signs
    • Check understanding.
    • Help the client choose an appropriate method using the family planning medical eligibility criteria wheel. See summary of the wheel in section 15.1.10.
    • Explain next steps needed.

    15.1.7 Perform a Physical Assessment¶

    Perform a physical assessment and pay particular attention to:

    • Jaundice
    • Anaemia
    • Enlarged glands
    • Goitre
    • Breasts, for example lumps
    • Abdomen, for example enlarged organs such as liver or uterus

    15.1.8 Perform a Pelvic Examination¶

    The following areas need to be investigated:

    • Inspect external genitalia.
    • Perform speculum examination.
    • Perform cervical cancer screening, such as VIA, VILI, or Pap smear.
    • Perform bimanual examination to determine size of uterus for comparison later.
    • Share findings with the client in simple language.
    • Explain next steps needed.
    • Advise on when to have next examination, for example routine, annual, follow-up, or if problems occur.

    15.1.9 Manage Client for Chosen FP Method¶

    • Use the table in section 15.1.10 to quickly assess suitability of the method being considered.
    • Provide a suitable method and ensure the client fully understands how the method works.
    • Explain how any medicine for home use is to be taken.
    • Advise the client on potential problems with the chosen method and when to return immediately.
    • Discuss management of serious side effects and complications.
    • Arrange for the client to return for routine follow-up and additional family planning supplies.

    15.2 Overview of Key Contraceptive Methods¶

    The following sections contain an overview of mainstream contraceptive methods and how to manage side effects if they occur.

    Side effects are one of the most common reasons why women stop using contraception. The health worker should be able to counsel the patient and address her concerns appropriately.

    15.2.1 Condom (Male)¶

    ICD-10 CODE: Z30.0 / Z30.49

    Indications

    Male condoms are indicated for:

    • Couples needing an immediately effective method
    • Clients who prefer this family planning method
    • Couples waiting to rule out suspected pregnancy
    • Protection against exposure to STIs, including HIV/AIDS
    • Situations where a backup method is needed, for example when a woman is starting oral contraceptives or has forgotten to take oral contraceptives
    • Couples where one or both partners have HIV/AIDS, even if using another family planning method

    Advantages

    • Enables the male partner to play a role in family planning
    • Protects against unwanted pregnancy
    • Protects against STIs and HIV infection

    Disadvantages

    • Some men may have difficulty maintaining an erection with a condom on.
    • May cause reduced penile sensitivity.
    • Occasional hypersensitivity to latex or lubricants may occur and may result in a severe allergic reaction.
    • Requires correct use with every act of sex for greatest effectiveness.

    Management

    Instructions LOC
    Ensure the client understands correct use, storage, and disposal of condoms.

    Supply at least 100 condoms to each client for 3 months and, if available, a water-based or silicone-based lubricant.

    Advise the client to return for more before condoms are finished.

    In case of hypersensitivity to latex or lubricants, avoid latex-based condoms and use the female condom or another family planning method.    		 HC2

    15.2.2 Condom (Female)¶

    ICD-10 CODE: Z30.0 / Z30.49

    Examples include Femidom, Care, and FC2.

    The female condom is a soft plastic pre-lubricated sheath with an inner and outer ring. It is inserted into the vagina before sexual intercourse.

    Indications

    Female condoms are indicated:

    • As for male condoms above
    • For women whose partners will not use male condoms
    • Where the male partner has allergy or sensitivity to latex condoms

    Advantages

    • Woman-controlled, but requires partner cooperation
    • Can be inserted hours before intercourse and therefore does not interrupt sexual spontaneity
    • Not dependent on male erection
    • Does not require immediate withdrawal after ejaculation
    • Protects against STIs and HIV infection
    • Requires no special storage

    Disadvantages and side effects

    • Requires special training and practice to use correctly
    • Relatively new product with limited public awareness
    • Hypersensitivity to polyurethane female condoms may occur in some cases
    • Requires correct use with every act of sex for greatest effectiveness

    Management

    Instructions LOC
    Ensure the client understands correct use, storage, and disposal.

    Supply at least 40 female condoms to each client per month.

    Advise the client to return for more before they are finished.

    In case of hypersensitivity, avoid use and change to another family planning method.    		 HC2

    15.2.3 Combined Oral Contraceptive Pill (COC)¶

    ICD-10 CODE: Z30.0 / Z30.41

    Indications

    Combined oral contraceptive pills are indicated for:

    • Women below 35 years needing a highly effective family planning method
    • Non-breastfeeding clients
    • Breastfeeding clients after 6 months postpartum
    • Clients with dysmenorrhoea
    • Clients with heavy periods or ovulation pain
    • Clients concerned by irregular menstrual cycles

    Contraindications

    Do not use COCs in clients with:

    • Diastolic blood pressure above 100 mmHg
    • Cardiac disease
    • Thromboembolic disease, for example deep vein thrombosis
    • Active liver disease
    • Less than 6 months after childbirth
    • Major surgery planned within 4 weeks
    • Unexplained abnormal vaginal bleeding
    • Known or suspected cervical cancer
    • Undiagnosed breast lumps or breast cancer
    • Known or suspected pregnancy

    Risk factors

    If any 2 of the following are present, recommend a progestogen-only or non-hormonal family planning method:

    • Smoking, especially more than 10 cigarettes per day
    • Age above 35 years
    • Diabetes

    Advantages and other potential health benefits

    COCs protect against:

    • Unwanted pregnancy
    • Cancer of the ovary or lining of the uterus
    • Symptomatic pelvic inflammatory disease

    COCs may reduce:

    • Menstrual cramps and bleeding problems
    • Ovulation pain
    • Excess hair on the body or face
    • Acne
    • Symptoms of polycystic ovarian syndrome

    Disadvantages and common side effects

    • Does not protect against STIs
    • Spotting, nausea, and vomiting within the first few months
    • Changes in bleeding patterns, including fewer days of bleeding, irregular bleeding, lighter bleeding, infrequent bleeding, or no monthly bleeding
    • Headaches
    • Dizziness
    • Weight gain
    • Effectiveness depends on regular daily dosage
    • Mood changes
    • Breast tenderness
    • Suppression of lactation

    Medicine interactions

    Some medicines reduce COC effectiveness, including medicines that increase hepatic enzyme activity, such as:

    • Rifampicin, especially
    • Carbamazepine
    • Griseofulvin
    • Nevirapine
    • Phenytoin
    • Phenobarbital

    Short courses of some broad-spectrum antibiotics may also reduce effectiveness, for example:

    • Ampicillin
    • Amoxicillin
    • Doxycycline

    Note

    An additional family planning method must be used during treatment with these medicines and for at least 7 days after completion.

    Complications and warning signs

    Advise the client to return immediately or seek care if she develops:

    • Severe headaches or blurred vision
    • Depression
    • Acute severe abdominal pain
    • Chest pain with dyspnoea, which may suggest pulmonary embolism
    • Swelling or pain in calf muscle, which may suggest deep vein thrombosis

    Management

    Instructions LOC
    Give 3 cycles of COC and explain carefully:

    - How to take the tablets
    - That strict compliance is essential
    - What to do if doses are missed
    - What to do if side effects or warning signs occur

    If starting COC within 5 days of the period:

    - Supply and show how to use a backup family planning method.
    - Ask the client to return when fewer than 7 tablets remain in the last cycle.    		 HC2

    Management of side effects of COCs

    Side effect Management
    Nausea Assess for pregnancy and malaria.

    Suggest taking COCs at bedtime or with food.

    Advise the client to take the pill at the same time daily.

    If symptoms continue, consider locally available remedies such as eating roasted grains, roasted cassava, or boiled greens.
    Breast tenderness Assess for pregnancy.

    Recommend wearing a supportive bra.

    Examine for breast infection, lumps, nipple discharge, and other cancer symptoms.

    If breastfeeding, examine for breast infection.

    If there is infection, use warm compresses and refer for appropriate evaluation.

    If examination shows a suspicious lump or discharge, refer for appropriate evaluation.

    Counsel on non-hormonal family planning methods.

    Try hot or cold compresses.

    Suggest ibuprofen, paracetamol, or other pain relievers.
    Mild headaches Take a proper history, including when headaches occur, whether the client can continue daily tasks, and what medicines relieve the headaches.

    Take blood pressure.

    If blood pressure is normal, give pain relievers such as ibuprofen or paracetamol.

    If headaches get worse or occur more often, refer for appropriate evaluation.
    Palpitations Rule out anaemia and check blood pressure and weight.

    Reassure the client that this is common in COC users and usually disappears in a few months.

    Evaluate for other causes unrelated to the method and refer if necessary.
    Chest pain Evaluate the cause and refer if necessary.

    15.2.4 Progestogen-Only Pill (POP)¶

    ICD-10 CODE: Z30.0 / Z30.41

    Progestogen-only pills contain very low doses of a progestin similar to the natural hormone progesterone in a woman’s body.

    Since these pills do not contain oestrogen, they are safe to use throughout breastfeeding and by women who cannot use methods containing oestrogen.

    Indications

    Progestogen-only pills are indicated for:

    • Breastfeeding and non-breastfeeding clients immediately postpartum
    • Women who cannot take COCs but prefer to use pills
    • Women of all ages who desire contraceptive pills

    Contraindications

    Do not use POPs in clients with:

    • Known or suspected breast or genital malignancy
    • Known or suspected pregnancy
    • Breast cancer more than 5 years ago, if it has not recurred
    • Severe liver disease, infection, or tumour
    • Undiagnosed vaginal bleeding
    • Current or previous blood clot
    • High blood pressure
    • Systemic lupus erythematosus with positive or unknown antiphospholipid antibodies

    Use caution where the client is taking medicines that reduce POP effectiveness, including:

    • Barbiturates
    • Carbamazepine
    • Oxcarbazepine
    • Phenytoin
    • Primidone
    • Topiramate
    • Rifampicin
    • Rifabutin
    • Ritonavir or ritonavir-boosted protease inhibitors

    A backup contraceptive method should be used when these medicines are being taken.

    Disadvantages and common side effects

    • Does not protect against STIs
    • Spotting
    • Amenorrhoea
    • Unpredictable irregular periods
    • Not as effective as COCs
    • Effectiveness is reduced by medicines that increase hepatic enzyme activity

    Management

    Instructions LOC
    Give 3 cycles of POP.

    Explain carefully how to take the tablets and what to do if doses are missed or if side effects occur.

    Supply and show how to use a backup family planning method for the first 14 days of the first packet, for example condoms or abstinence from sex.

    Ask the client to return 11 weeks after starting POP.

    Use the last pill packet to show when this return visit will be.    		 HC2

    Management of side effects of POPs

    Side effect Management
    No monthly periods Assess for pregnancy.

    If not pregnant and/or breastfeeding, reassure the client that some women using POPs stop having monthly periods and that this is not harmful.

    If pregnant, reassure the client that POPs will not affect the pregnancy and refer her to ANC.
    Nausea or dizziness Suggest taking POPs at bedtime or with food.

    If symptoms continue, consider locally available remedies.
    Migraine or headaches If migraine is without aura, such as without hallucinations or hearing voices, the client may continue using POPs voluntarily.

    If migraine is with aura, stop POPs and help the client choose a method without hormones.
    Irregular bleeding Assess for pregnancy or abortion.

    Reassure the client that many women using POPs get irregular bleeding whether breastfeeding or not. It is not harmful and should lessen or stop after several months of use.

    Counsel on how to reduce irregular bleeding, for example making up for missed pills after vomiting or diarrhoea.

    If bleeding continues, give ibuprofen 400 to 800 mg every 8 hours after meals for 5 days when irregular bleeding starts.

    Or give mefenamic acid 500 mg three times daily for 5 to 7 days.

    Check for anaemia and treat accordingly.

    If irregular bleeding persists or starts after several months of normal or no monthly bleeding, investigate other reasons unrelated to POPs and treat accordingly.

    Change to another pill formulation for at least 3 months or help the client choose another family planning method.
    Heavy or prolonged bleeding Heavy or prolonged bleeding means twice as much bleeding as usual or bleeding longer than 8 days.

    Assess for pregnancy or abortion.

    Reassure and comfort the client.

    Give ibuprofen 800 mg every 8 hours after meals for 5 days when bleeding starts.

    Or give another NSAID.

    Give ferrous salt tablets containing 60 mg iron to prevent anaemia.

    Educate on nutrition.

    If heavy bleeding persists, investigate other reasons unrelated to POPs and treat accordingly.

    Change to another pill formulation for at least 3 months or help the client choose another family planning method, preferably COC if there is no contraindication.

    15.2.5 Injectable Progestogen-Only Contraceptive¶

    ICD-10 CODE: Z30.0 / Z30.42

    This is a slowly absorbed depot intramuscular or subcutaneous injection that provides contraceptive protection.

    Indications

    Injectable progestogen-only contraceptives are indicated for:

    • Fertile women requiring contraception
    • Breastfeeding postpartum women
    • Known or suspected HIV-positive women who need an effective family planning method
    • Women with sickle-cell disease
    • Women who cannot use COCs because of oestrogen content
    • Women who do not want more children but do not yet want voluntary surgical contraception
    • Women awaiting surgical contraception

    Contraindications

    Contraindications are as for POPs above.

    Advantages and other health benefits

    • Do not require daily action, such as taking pills
    • Do not interfere with sex
    • Private method; no one else can tell that a woman is using contraception
    • Cause no monthly bleeding in many women
    • Can be stopped at any time
    • DMPA reduces cancer of the lining of the uterus
    • DMPA reduces heavy flow in uterine fibroids
    • NET-EN reduces iron-deficiency anaemia

    Disadvantages and common side effects

    • Does not protect against STIs
    • Amenorrhoea
    • Amenorrhoea often occurs after the first injection and after 9 to 12 months of use
    • May cause heavy or prolonged vaginal bleeding during the first 1 to 2 months after injection
    • Weight gain
    • Loss of libido
    • May delay return to fertility for up to 12 months after stopping injection

    Complications and warning signs

    Advise the client to return immediately if she develops:

    • Headaches
    • Heavy vaginal bleeding
    • Severe abdominal pain
    • Excessive weight gain

    Management

    Instructions LOC
    Medroxyprogesterone acetate depot injection, IM

    Give 150 mg deep IM into the deltoid or buttock muscle.

    Do not rub the area because this increases absorption and shortens depot effect.

    Medroxyprogesterone acetate depot injection, SC

    Inject 104 mg into fatty tissue at the front of the thigh, back of the upper arm, or abdomen.

    This can be administered at community level.    		 HC1
    If given after day 1 to 7 of the menstrual cycle, advise the client to abstain from sex or use a backup family planning method, such as condoms, for the first 7 days after injection.

    Advise the client to return for the next dose on a specific date, 12 weeks after the injection.

    If the client returns more than 2 to 4 weeks later than the advised date, rule out pregnancy before giving the next dose.

    Counsel on likely side effects and advise the client to return promptly if warning signs occur.    		 HC1

    Management of side effects of injectable progestogen-only contraceptives

    Side effect Management
    No monthly period Assess for pregnancy.

    If pregnant, reassure the client that injectable progestogen-only contraceptive will not affect the pregnancy and refer her to ANC.

    If not pregnant, reassure her that this contraceptive may stop monthly periods and that this is not harmful. She can continue with the method or choose another.
    Irregular bleeding Assess for pregnancy or abortion.

    Reassure the client that many women using injectable progestogen-only contraceptives have irregular bleeding. It is not harmful in the first few months and should lessen or stop after a few months.

    If irregular bleeding continues, give ibuprofen 400 to 800 mg 8 hourly when irregular bleeding starts.

    Or give mefenamic acid 500 mg 8 hourly after meals for 5 days.

    Avoid tranexamic acid for bleeding due to contraceptive use because of concern for blood clots.

    If irregular bleeding continues or starts after several months of normal or no monthly bleeding, investigate other reasons unrelated to the contraceptive and treat accordingly.

    Help the client choose another family planning method if necessary.
    Heavy bleeding Heavy bleeding includes blood clots, bleeding that interferes with daily routine, bleeding for more than 7 days, or feeling thirsty all the time.

    If heavy bleeding occurs between 8 and 12 weeks after the first injection, assess for pregnancy or abortion, reassure as for irregular bleeding, repeat the progestogen-only injection, and change the return date to 3 months after the latest injection.

    If heavy bleeding occurs after the second injection, assess for pregnancy or abortion, reassure and comfort the client, and give 1 COC pill daily for 21 days.

    If heavy bleeding occurs after the third or later injection, assess for pregnancy or abortion, reassure and comfort the client, give 1 COC pill daily for 21 days when bleeding starts, or give ethinyl estradiol 50 micrograms daily for 21 days.

    Give ibuprofen 800 mg 8 hourly or mefenamic acid 500 mg 8 hourly after meals for 5 days.

    Give ferrous salt tablets containing 60 mg iron to prevent anaemia.

    If bleeding persists, investigate other reasons unrelated to injectable contraceptives and treat accordingly. Help the client choose another family planning method if necessary.
    Delayed return to fertility Reassure the woman that she should not be worried if she has not become pregnant even after stopping use for 12 months.

    Counsel her about fertile days. Ovulation normally occurs 14 days before the next menstrual period if the woman has a 28-day regular cycle.
    Weight gain Rule out pregnancy.

    Ask about diet, exercise, and eating habits that promote weight gain. Counsel as needed.

    Explain that all hormonal contraceptives may have a slight effect on weight.

    If weight gain is more than 2 kg, counsel on diet and exercise.
    Loss of libido Take a proper history.

    Find out whether there is stress, fatigue, anxiety, depression, new medication, dry vagina, or painful intercourse.

    Explore lifestyle and suggest changes where needed.

    Advise on foreplay and, where possible, involve the spouse.

    Help the client choose another family planning method if necessary.
    Headache Explore possible social, financial, health, or physical causes of headache.

    Ask the client to keep a record of the timing and number of headaches for the next 2 weeks and return for follow-up.

    Evaluate the cause of headache, including raised blood pressure or sinus infection.

    Give pain relievers such as acetylsalicylic acid, ibuprofen, or paracetamol.

    Regardless of age, a woman who develops migraine headaches with aura, or whose migraine headaches become worse while using monthly injections, should stop using the injectable. If migraine headaches are without aura, she can continue using the method if she wishes.

    15.2.6 Progestogen-Only Sub-Dermal Implant¶

    ICD-10 CODE: Z30.0 / Z30.46

    A progestogen-only sub-dermal implant is a long-acting contraceptive inserted under the skin of the upper arm.

    Indications

    Progestogen-only implants may be used by women who need long-acting contraception where alternative family planning methods are inappropriate or undesirable.

    Contraindications

    Contraindications are as for progestogen-only pills.

    Advantages and health benefits

    • Highly effective, with only 1% to 3% failure rate
    • No delay in return to fertility after removal
    • Long-acting
    • Low user responsibility; no need for daily action
    • Protects against symptomatic pelvic inflammatory disease

    Disadvantages and common side effects

    • Does not protect against STIs
    • Irregular bleeding, spotting, or heavy bleeding in the first few months
    • Amenorrhoea
    • Possibility of local infection at insertion site
    • Must be surgically inserted and removed by a specially trained service provider
    • May not be as effective in women above 70 kg

    Warning signs

    Advise the client to return urgently if she develops:

    • Heavy vaginal bleeding
    • Severe chest pain
    • Pus, bleeding, or pain at insertion site on arm

    Management

    Instructions LOC
    Insert the implant subdermally under the skin of the upper arm following recommended procedures. HC2
    Carefully explain warning signs and the need to return if they occur.

    Advise the client to return:

    - After 2 weeks to examine the implant site
    - After 3 months for first routine follow-up
    - Annually until the implant is removed    		 HC2

    Management of side effects of implants

    Side effect Management
    No monthly periods Assess for pregnancy.

    If pregnant, reassure the client that the implant will not affect the pregnancy and refer her to ANC.

    If not pregnant, reassure her that implants may stop monthly periods and that this is not harmful. She can continue with the method.
    Irregular bleeding If irregular bleeding continues, give ibuprofen 400 to 800 mg 8 hourly when irregular bleeding starts.

    Or give mefenamic acid 500 mg 8 hourly after meals for 5 days.

    Check for anaemia and treat accordingly.

    If bleeding persists, give 1 COC pill daily for 21 days, or ethinyl estradiol 50 micrograms daily for 21 days.

    Investigate other reasons unrelated to implants and treat accordingly.

    Help the client choose another family planning method.
    Heavy or prolonged bleeding Heavy or prolonged bleeding means twice as much as usual or longer than 8 days.

    Assess for pregnancy or abortion and reassure the client.

    Give ibuprofen 800 mg 8 hourly when irregular bleeding starts.

    Or give mefenamic acid 500 mg 8 hourly after meals for 5 days.

    Give 1 COC pill daily for 21 days.

    Give ferrous salt tablets containing 60 mg iron to prevent anaemia.

    Educate on nutrition.

    If bleeding persists, investigate other reasons unrelated to implants and treat accordingly. Help the client choose another family planning method.
    Weight gain Manage the same as for injectable progestogen-only contraceptives.
    Loss of libido Manage the same as for injectable progestogen-only contraceptives.
    Infection at insertion site Do not remove the implant initially.

    Clean the infected area with soap and water or antiseptic.

    Give oral antibiotics for 7 to 10 days, such as amoxicillin 500 mg 8 hourly.

    If there is no improvement after 10 days, refer.

    Ask the client to return after taking all antibiotics if the infection does not clear. If infection has not cleared, remove the implant or refer for removal.

    Expulsion or partial expulsion often follows infection. Ask the client to return if she notices an implant coming out.
    Migraine headaches If migraine headaches are without aura, she may continue to use the implant if she wishes.

    If migraine aura is present, remove the implant and help her choose a method without hormones.

    15.2.7 Emergency Contraception (Pill and IUD)¶

    ICD-10 CODE: Z30.012

    Emergency contraception can be used to prevent unwanted pregnancy after unprotected sex, rape, defilement, or contraceptive method failure.

    Available methods include:

    • Emergency contraceptive pills
    • Emergency contraceptive IUD

    Note

    Emergency contraceptive methods do not cause abortion.

    Regular users of emergency contraceptive pills should be counselled to use a routine contraceptive method.

    Type Features
    Emergency contraceptive pill Contains a special dose of progestin, levonorgestrel.

    May come as one pill of 1.5 mg or two pills of 0.75 mg.

    The dose of 1.5 mg should be taken as soon as possible within 72 hours, but can be taken up to 5 days after unprotected sex or contraceptive method failure, such as condom burst, failure to take regular family planning methods, or rape.

    Emergency contraceptive pills are not regular contraceptive pills and should not be used as a routine family planning method.
    Emergency contraceptive IUD Should be inserted as soon as possible after penetrative sexual intercourse, but within 5 days.

    Monitor for side effects as outlined below.

    Indications

    Emergency contraception is indicated for:

    • All women and adolescents at risk of becoming pregnant after unprotected sex

    Advantages

    • Prevents unplanned pregnancy after penetrative sexual intercourse
    • Safe for all women
    • Has no long-term side effects
    • Does not cause infertility
    • Can be kept on hand in case of emergency
    • Controlled by the woman

    Disadvantages and side effects

    • Does not protect against STIs
    • May be misused as a regular contraceptive method
    • May cause minor short-term side effects such as:
    • Nausea and vomiting
    • Altered menstrual bleeding
    • Headache
    • Abdominal pain
    • Breast tenderness
    • Dizziness
    • Fatigue

    Management

    Instructions LOC
    Emergency contraception should be taken as soon as possible after unprotected sex where pregnancy is not desired.

    It can prevent pregnancy if taken any time within 5 days after unprotected sex, although efficacy decreases over this 5-day window.

    It is safe and suitable for all women at risk of unplanned pregnancy.

    Women on ARVs should take a double dose, for example levonorgestrel 3 mg, equivalent to Postinor 4 tablets.    		 HC2
    Warn women against regular or frequent use of emergency contraception.

    Advise them to consider using other long-term methods.    		 HC2

    15.2.8 Intrauterine Device (IUD)¶

    ICD-10 CODE: Z30.014

    An intrauterine device is an easily reversible long-term family planning method. It is effective for up to 10 years and can be inserted as soon as 6 weeks postpartum.

    Types include:

    • Non-hormonal IUD: copper-loaded
    • Hormonal IUD: levonorgestrel-loaded

    Indications

    IUDs are indicated for:

    • Women desiring long-term contraception
    • Breastfeeding mothers
    • Women for whom hormonal family planning methods are contraindicated
    • Treatment of heavy periods or menorrhagia, for levonorgestrel-loaded IUD

    Contraindications

    Do not use IUDs in clients with:

    • Known or suspected pregnancy
    • PID or history of PID in the last 3 months
    • Undiagnosed abnormal uterine bleeding
    • Risk of STIs
    • Reduced immunity, for example diabetes mellitus or terminal AIDS
    • Known or suspected cancer of pelvic organs
    • Severe anaemia or heavy menstrual bleeding

    Advantages

    • Prevents unplanned pregnancy after penetrative sexual intercourse
    • Can be used as emergency contraception
    • Safe for all women, including breastfeeding mothers
    • Does not affect libido, especially copper IUD
    • Long-term method
    • 97% to 99% effective
    • Levonorgestrel IUD reduces chances of getting STIs
    • Recommended for women with non-communicable diseases such as diabetes and hypertension
    • Does not increase the risk of STIs

    Disadvantages and common side effects

    • Mild cramps during the first 3 to 5 days after insertion
    • Longer and heavier menstrual blood loss in the first 3 months
    • Vaginal discharge in the first 3 months
    • Spotting or bleeding between periods
    • Increased cramping pains during menstruation
    • Threads may prick the spouse during sex; cut the threads shorter if needed

    Complications and warning signs

    Advise the client to return immediately if she develops:

    • Lower abdominal pain or PID
    • Foul-smelling vaginal discharge
    • Missed period
    • Displaced IUD or missing strings
    • Prolonged vaginal bleeding
    • Suspected perforation

    Management

    Instructions LOC
    Insert the IUD closely following recommended procedures.

    Explain each step to the client.

    Ensure the thread is cut short enough not to cause discomfort.

    Carefully explain possible side effects and what to do if they arise.    		 HC3
    Advise the client:

    - To avoid vaginal douching.
    - Not to have more than one sexual partner.
    - To check each sanitary pad before disposal to ensure the IUD has not been expelled. If expelled, she should use an alternative family planning method and return to the clinic.
    - How to check that the IUD is still in place after each menstruation.
    - To report promptly to the clinic if there is late period or pregnancy, abdominal pain during intercourse, exposure to STI, feeling unwell with chills or fever, shorter, longer, or missing strings, or feeling the hard part of the IUD in the vagina or at the cervix.
    - To use condoms if there is any risk of STIs including HIV.

    Recommend a follow-up visit after 3 to 6 weeks to check on the client.    		 HC3

    Management of side effects of IUD

    Side effect Management
    No monthly period Assess for pregnancy.

    If pregnant, reassure that the IUD will not affect the pregnancy and refer her to ANC.

    If not pregnant, investigate other reasons for amenorrhoea.

    If there is no pregnancy, reassure the client.
    Irregular bleeding Assess for pregnancy or abortion.

    Reassure that many women using IUDs get irregular bleeding. It is not harmful and should lessen or stop after several months of use.

    If bleeding continues, give ibuprofen 400 to 800 mg 8 hourly after meals for 5 days when irregular bleeding starts.

    Give tranexamic acid 500 mg 8 hourly for 5 to 7 days.

    Check for anaemia and treat accordingly.

    If irregular bleeding persists, investigate other reasons unrelated to the IUD and treat accordingly. Help the client choose another family planning method if necessary.
    Heavy bleeding Assess for pregnancy or abortion.

    Give ibuprofen 400 to 800 mg every 8 hours after meals for 5 days.

    Or give tranexamic acid 1,500 mg every 8 hours for 3 days, then 1,000 mg once daily for 2 days.

    Give ferrous salt tablets containing 60 mg iron to prevent anaemia.

    Educate on nutrition.

    If bleeding persists, investigate other reasons unrelated to the IUD and treat accordingly. Help the client choose another family planning method if necessary.

    15.2.9 Natural FP: Cervical Mucus Method (CMM) and Moon Beads¶

    ICD-10 CODE: Z30.02

    The cervical mucus method is a fertility awareness-based method of family planning. It relies on changes in the nature of vaginal mucus during the menstrual cycle to detect the fertile time.

    During the fertile time, the couple avoids pregnancy by changing sexual behaviour through:

    • Abstaining from sexual intercourse, also called periodic abstinence
    • Using barrier methods, for example condoms or cervical caps

    Guidance on correct use of the method is available only at centres with specially trained service providers.

    Management

    Instructions LOC
    Ensure the client understands how the method works.

    Explain how to distinguish the different types of mucus.

    Show the client how to complete the CMM chart, which can be used together with moon beads.

    Carry out a practice or trial period of at least 3 cycles.

    Confirm that the chart is correctly filled.

    Advise the client to always use condoms as well as CMM if there is any risk of exposure to STIs or HIV.

    Advise the client to return on a specific follow-up date after one menstrual cycle.    		 HC1

    15.2.10 Natural FP: Lactational Amenorrhoea Method (LAM)¶

    ICD-10 CODE: Z30.02

    The lactational amenorrhoea method relies on suppression of ovulation through exclusive breastfeeding as a means of contraception.

    Guidance on correct use of this method is available only at centres with trained service providers.

    LAM requires all 3 of the following conditions to be met:

    • The mother’s monthly bleeding has not returned.
    • The baby is fully or nearly fully breastfed and is fed often, day and night.
    • The baby is less than 6 months old.

    Disadvantages

    • Does not protect against STIs
    • Provides low couple-years of protection

    Management

    Instructions LOC
    Ensure the client understands how the method works.

    Explain that she must breastfeed the child on demand on both breasts at least 10 to 12 times during the day and night, including at least once nightly in the first months.

    Daytime feedings should not be more than 4 hours apart, and night-time feedings should not be more than 6 hours apart.

    She must not give the child any solid foods or other liquids apart from breast milk.    		 HC1
    Advise the client that LAM will no longer be an effective family planning method if:

    - The baby does not feed regularly on demand.
    - Menstruation resumes, in which case she will need another family planning method.

    Advise the client:

    - To use condoms as well as LAM if there is any risk of exposure to STIs or HIV.
    - To return after 3 months for routine follow-up or earlier if she has any problem.
    - To return if she wants to change to another family planning method.    		 HC1

    15.2.11 Surgical Contraception for Men: Vasectomy¶

    ICD-10 CODE: Z30.2

    Vasectomy is a permanent family planning method involving a minor operation under local anaesthetic to cut and tie the two sperm-carrying tubes, the vas deferens.

    It is available only at centres with specially trained service providers.

    There is need to dispel myths of impotence following vasectomy.

    Indications

    Vasectomy is indicated for:

    • Fully aware and counselled clients who have voluntarily signed the consent form
    • Males in couples who have definitely reached their desired family size and want no more children
    • Males in couples where the woman cannot risk another pregnancy due to age or health problems

    Management

    Instructions LOC
    Ensure the client understands how the method works and that it is permanent, not reversible, and highly effective.

    Explain that vasectomy is not castration and sexual ability or activity is not affected.

    Explain that the procedure is not immediately effective and that the client will need to use a condom for at least 15 ejaculations after the operation or for 3 months.    		 HC4
    After the operation, advise the client:

    - On wound care.
    - To return for routine follow-up after 7 days or earlier if there is fever, excessive swelling, pus, or tenderness at the operation site.
    - To continue using condoms or other contraceptive devices for 3 months after the procedure.
    - To use condoms if there is any risk of HIV or STIs.    		 HC4

    15.2.12 Surgical Contraception for Women: Tubal Ligation¶

    ICD-10 CODE: Z30.2

    Tubal ligation is a permanent family planning method involving a minor 15-minute operation under local anaesthetic to cut and tie the two egg-carrying fallopian tubes.

    It is available only at centres with specially trained service providers.

    Indications

    Indications are as for vasectomy above, but for females.

    Management

    Instructions LOC
    Ensure the client understands how the method works and that it is permanent, irreversible, highly effective, and immediately effective.

    Explain that there may be some discomfort or pain over the small wound for a few days.

    Advise the client:

    - On wound care.
    - To use condoms if there is any risk of exposure to STIs or HIV.
    - To return after 7 days for routine follow-up or earlier if there is fever, excessive swelling, pus, or tenderness at the operation site.    		 HC4
